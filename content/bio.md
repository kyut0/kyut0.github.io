...and thanks for stopping by.

I started out at the University of Oklahoma as a **geographic information science**
student, learning to see the world in layers: ArcGIS, ENVI, and Landsat scenes stacked into
maps. An internship with NASA DEVELOP had me tracing 30 years of red spruce change from
satellite imagery, and I was hooked on what spatial data could **reveal**.

Then I found the other half of my story: data at scale. Alongside geography, I studied
Economics and Big Data, earning two bachelor's degrees and a master's in five years, and
**code became the first tool I reached for**. At E. & J. Gallo Winery, I got to do some genuinely
cool science: I designed a method in Google Earth Engine and R that read vineyard health
from satellite imagery and tied it to irrigation. Between organizing a sprawling file system
and heading into the rows to sample the grapes, it was my first **taste of science** that ran
from the satellite to the vine. At UT Austin's Bureau of Economic Geology, I wrangled large
oil and gas datasets in R to map unconventional drilling across the US, and shared the
results in papers, posters, and talks.

Meanwhile, the field kept moving, and so did I. Geospatial scientists are now expected to
do more than make maps: to build the pipelines, ship the code, and keep the data
trustworthy end to end. At Grassroots Carbon, I grew from Carbon Technical Manager,
screening ranches with RShiny and QGIS, into a **Data Engineer**, going from Python novice
to production pipelines in less than a year and a half. Today I build Python pipeli
model soil carbon reductions and removals, containerize them with Docker, deploy them with
cron and GitHub Actata with Pydantic. I've processed a dozen datasets onto Uber's H3 hexagonal
index as Parquet files on AWS S3, and I review my colleagues' pull requests as carefully
as I rewrite my own code.

AI has changed how I work, too. Along with writing well over ten thousand lines of code by
hand, I lean on Claude, Gemini, and Copilot to move faster, and placed in the top 5 of 27
in a company-wide AI competition. The tools will keep changing, and **I plan to keep
learning faster than they do**.

My mantra, in life and at work, is "**I can do hard things**." I live it daily by meditating,
cold plunging, weightlifting, and keeping habits I track in a personal app I built for the
purpose. I do my best work surrounded by smart, hardworking people who treat others with
respect and share the same goals.
